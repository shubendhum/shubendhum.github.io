The Art of Stakeholder Management in Identity Engineering

Author Shubendhu Mahajan Published on ·

Mon Dec 23, 2024

·

855 Words

As a senior engineering leader in the Identity domain, I’ve come to appreciate one truth: delivering successful outcomes isn’t just about technical expertise—it’s about building strong, collaborative relationships with stakeholders. From compliance teams to senior executives, each stakeholder plays a vital role in the success of identity projects. However, managing these relationships requires a nuanced approach to communication, understanding their needs, and tailoring your message accordingly.

In this blog, I’ll share my perspective on stakeholder management and how it has been a cornerstone of smooth identity delivery, drawn from my practical experience leading cross-functional initiatives.

Understanding Your Stakeholders

The first step in effective stakeholder management is understanding the diverse perspectives and expertise of those you work with. In my role, stakeholders include:

  • Compliance and Security Teams: Highly technical, focused on risk, regulatory alignment, and system integrity.
  • CX/Design Teams: Creative and user-focused, prioritizing seamless customer experiences.
  • Front-End and SRE Teams: Deeply technical, requiring clear, detailed communication on system behavior and dependencies.
  • Business Owners and Project Managers: Outcome-oriented, requiring a balance of technical insight and business value.
  • Senior Executives: Strategic, focusing on the broader impact, timelines, and ROI rather than technical intricacies.

Recognizing the unique priorities of each group ensures that communication is relevant and impactful.

Tailoring the Message to the Audience

One of the most valuable lessons I’ve learned is that the same message can’t be delivered the same way to everyone. Here’s how I’ve approached tailoring communication:

**a. Technical Engagement for Experts
When working with highly technical teams like Security, SRE, or Front-End Engineering, I dive deep into the details. Discussions often include:

  • Protocol choices (e.g., OAuth, SAML).
  • Threat models and mitigations.
  • System architecture diagrams and their implications.

For instance, when addressing the SRE team, I might focus on system uptime, scalability, and operational resilience, providing specific metrics or examples of load testing outcomes.

**b. Simplifying for Business Stakeholders
For business owners or project managers, technical jargon can be overwhelming. Instead, I focus on translating technical progress into business outcomes:

  • How a feature aligns with customer needs.
  • Expected improvements in NPS or customer satisfaction.
  • Timelines and risk mitigation strategies.

When discussing compliance changes, I avoid diving into encryption algorithms and instead frame the conversation around meeting regulatory deadlines and avoiding potential fines.

**c. Strategic Context for Executives
Senior executives don’t need every detail—they need the bigger picture. I focus on:

  • How identity initiatives align with organizational goals.
  • The financial impact of fraud reduction or customer retention.
  • Key risks, mitigations, and resource needs.

In one instance, while presenting a roadmap to executives, I used a customer journey map to show how identity features would improve onboarding times, highlighting the anticipated revenue impact.

Building Relationships Through Trust and Transparency

Strong relationships aren’t built overnight. They require trust, transparency, and consistent engagement. Here’s what has worked for me:

  • Regular Check-Ins: Scheduling regular syncs with key stakeholders to align on priorities and progress.
  • Admitting Uncertainty: When I don’t have an immediate answer, I’m honest about it and commit to following up quickly.
  • Listening Actively: Understanding concerns, even when they conflict with technical priorities, is crucial to finding balanced solutions.

For example, a compliance request once delayed a critical release. By engaging early and explaining the technical implications, we collaboratively found a compromise that met their needs without jeopardizing the timeline.

Driving Alignment Across Diverse Teams

One of the greatest challenges is ensuring all stakeholders are aligned despite their differing perspectives. Here’s how I approach this:

  • Clear Documentation: Providing detailed technical specs for engineers while summarizing key takeaways for non-technical teams.
  • Cross-Functional Workshops: Hosting sessions where teams can share their views, fostering mutual understanding.
  • Unified Goals: Constantly tying conversations back to shared objectives, like improving security, user experience, or compliance readiness.

In one case, a CX team’s push for a seamless login experience conflicted with the Security team’s strict MFA requirements. By bringing both teams together, we co-designed a solution that balanced usability and security, creating a win-win outcome.

The Impact of Effective Stakeholder Management

Strong stakeholder management doesn’t just make projects easier to deliver—it ensures they succeed. Here’s what I’ve seen firsthand:

  • Faster Decision-Making: With trust and clear communication, approvals and buy-ins come quicker.
  • Higher-Quality Outcomes: Collaboration ensures that all perspectives are considered, leading to better solutions.
  • Stronger Relationships: Teams respect and value each other’s contributions, fostering long-term partnerships.

In one project, the alignment between compliance, design, and engineering teams was so effective that a complex identity feature launched three weeks ahead of schedule, with unanimous stakeholder approval.

Lessons Learned

If there’s one takeaway from my experience, it’s this: effective stakeholder management is about bridging gaps, building trust, and tailoring communication to each audience.

For anyone leading cross-functional teams, I recommend focusing on:

  • Understanding stakeholder needs deeply.
  • Adjusting your communication style to suit your audience.
  • Fostering collaboration through trust and transparency.

Identity delivery isn’t just a technical challenge—it’s a people challenge. And by managing those relationships well, you can achieve results that truly make an impact.

Strong relationships and tailored communication are the foundation of successful delivery. Whether you’re collaborating with engineers, business leaders, or executives, understanding their unique perspectives is the key to building trust and driving outcomes.
